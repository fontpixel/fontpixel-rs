# W5.5 oracle observations

The first unmodified monobit 0.54.0 read of our original three-glyph DRCS
fixture returned two glyphs. Its `softfont/dec.py::_read_drcs` converts the
terminator to an integer, while `_read_args` compares against byte strings and
only yields semicolon-terminated glyphs. DEC permits a final ST without a final
semicolon. Our reader handles both; our writer now includes a legal final
semicolon, which also interoperates with this released oracle. The original
failure was `KeyError: No glyph found matching label=0x43`.

Re-emitting the loaded DRCS font then failed in unmodified monobit with
`ValueError: not enough values to unpack (expected 3, got 2)`:
`_convert_to_drcs_props` unpacks `uprop.split('=')` into three variables. The test
records this exact exception and constructs a fresh `monobit.Font` from the
independently checked glyphs, with ASCII encoding and without the defective
native property string. That fresh font's standard DEC writer output is read
back by Rust and compared by pixels, BBX, advance and character codes. Monobit
writes all 94 slots and adds a matching G0 SCS designation; comparison includes
all original authored glyphs. Its expected-count warning on three-glyph input
is retained; DEC permits shorter complete downloads with erase control 0/2.
No monobit source or installed environment was modified.

The native LVGL v8.3.11 loader initially exposed a two-byte error in our binary
head field offsets: an omitted max-Y field in the layout calculation shifted
all descriptor bit widths. The C-runtime differential check failed on all three
glyphs, and the writer/reader field offsets were corrected. Both the upstream
loader and fmt_txt runtime now agree on all authored pixels, metrics and codes.
This failure demonstrated why self-roundtrips alone are insufficient.

During initial harness setup a generated `u8g2.h` fixture name collided with the
scratch copy of the upstream header. Compilation failed before execution. The
header was restored from upstream and fixture names now use a `fixture-` prefix.
Runtime provenance is recorded by SHA-256; no reference/sample or shared oracle
files were touched.
